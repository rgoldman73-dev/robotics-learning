## 2
topics: Optimization theory
read: PoRA Chapter 2; AA203 slides Lecture 2 and Recitation 2 (CVXPY)
mml: MML Ch 7.2-7.3 Constrained optimization, Lagrange multipliers and convex optimization
Q: State the first-order necessary condition for an unconstrained minimum. => The gradient is zero.
Q: What is the second-order condition? => The Hessian is positive semidefinite (positive definite for strict local minimum).
Q: What are Lagrange multipliers for? => Handling equality constraints: stationarity of L = f + λ^T g.
Q: What are the KKT conditions? => Stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility (λ ≥ 0 for inequalities) and complementary slackness.
Q: Why is convexity valuable? => Every local minimum is global and efficient solvers exist.
X: Use CVXPY (or scipy) to minimize ||Ax-b||^2 subject to x ≥ 0.
R: Optimality conditions (gradient, Hessian, KKT) are the vocabulary for all trajectory optimization.
## 3
topics: Calculus of variations
read: PoRA Chapter 2; AA203 slides Lecture 3
Q: What does calculus of variations optimize? => Functionals: functions of whole functions (e.g. the integral cost over a trajectory).
Q: State the Euler-Lagrange equation. => ∂L/∂x - d/dt(∂L/∂x_dot) = 0 for a stationary functional.
Q: What do variations/perturbations of a trajectory tell us? => Optimality requires the first variation to vanish for all admissible perturbations.
Q: How does it relate to optimal control? => Optimal control is calculus of variations with dynamic constraints, leading to adjoint (costate) equations.
X: Find the shortest path between two points (a straight line) by applying Euler-Lagrange to L=sqrt(1+y'^2).
R: Calculus of variations gives necessary conditions for optimal trajectories; it leads into indirect methods.
## 4
topics: Indirect methods for optimal control
read: PoRA Chapter 2; AA203 slides Lecture 4
Q: What is an indirect method? => First derive optimality conditions (e.g. Pontryagin), then solve the resulting boundary value problem.
Q: What is the Hamiltonian? => H = L(x,u) + λ^T f(x,u), combining running cost and dynamics via costates.
Q: What do costates represent? => Sensitivity of the optimal cost to the state.
Q: What makes the resulting problem a two-point boundary value problem? => States have initial conditions while costates have terminal conditions.
X: For x_dot=u, cost ∫u^2 dt, write H and the costate equation.
R: Indirect methods turn optimal control into solving the Hamiltonian conditions.
## 5
topics: Pontryagin's minimum principle, continuous-time LQR, computational methods
read: PoRA Chapters 2-3; AA203 slides Lecture 5 and code
Q: State Pontryagin's minimum principle. => The optimal control minimizes the Hamiltonian at every time (subject to constraints).
Q: What control law results for continuous-time LQR? => u = -K x with K = R^{-1} B^T P, P from the Riccati equation.
Q: What does a "bang-bang" control come from? => Hamiltonian that is linear in u, minimized at control bounds.
Q: Why do indirect methods struggle with computation? => Sensitive boundary value problems and poor initial guesses.
X: Solve LQR for a scalar system x_dot = a x + b u with cost ∫(q x^2 + r u^2) via the scalar Riccati equation.
R: Pontryagin gives necessary conditions; LQR is the key analytically solvable case.
## 6
topics: Direct methods for optimal control
read: PoRA Chapter 2; AA203 slides Lecture 6 and code
Q: What is a direct method? => Discretize states and controls and solve the resulting nonlinear program.
Q: Name two direct transcription approaches. => Direct collocation and (sequential) convex programming/single or multiple shooting.
Q: What is SCP? => Sequential convex programming: repeatedly convexify the problem around the current trajectory.
Q: Pros of direct vs indirect methods? => Easier to handle constraints and more robust to bad initial guesses, at the cost of larger problems.
X: Discretize x_dot = u over N=10 steps and write the constraint x_{k+1} = x_k + dt*u_k as a matrix.
R: Direct methods discretize and optimize; they are the practical workhorse for trajectory optimization.
## 7
topics: Dynamic programming and discrete-time LQR
read: PoRA Chapter 17 Sequential Decision Making and Dynamic Programming; AA203 slides Lecture 7
Q: State the principle of optimality. => Any tail of an optimal trajectory is optimal for the subproblem starting at that state.
Q: Write the Bellman recursion. => J_k(x) = min_u [ g(x,u) + J_{k+1}(f(x,u)) ].
Q: How does DP yield LQR? => With linear dynamics and quadratic cost, J_k(x) = x^T P_k x, giving a backward Riccati recursion and u_k = -K_k x.
Q: What is the curse of dimensionality? => Tabular DP grows exponentially with state dimension.
X: Implement the backward Riccati recursion for a double integrator in NumPy.
R: DP is the idea underneath RL; discrete LQR is its closed-form special case.
## 8
topics: Nonlinearity: tracking LQR, iterative LQR, differential dynamic programming
read: PoRA Chapter 3 Closed-Loop Control & Trajectory Tracking; AA203 slides Lecture 8 and code
Q: How do you use LQR for tracking a nominal trajectory? => Linearize around the trajectory, define error coordinates and apply time-varying LQR.
Q: What is iLQR? => Iterate: roll out, linearize, quadratically approximate cost, backward pass, forward pass with line search.
Q: What does DDP add? => Second-order terms of the dynamics.
Q: Why is LQR so widely used? => Efficient, stabilizing, handles multivariable systems; "implement it from memory" per the article.
X: Linearize the cart-pole around upright and compute K with scipy.linalg.solve_continuous_are.
R: LQR extends to nonlinear systems through linearization (tracking LQR, iLQR, DDP).
## 9
topics: Stochastic dynamic programming, value iteration, policy iteration
read: PoRA Chapter 17; AA203 slides Lecture 9
Q: How does stochastic DP differ from deterministic? => The Bellman equation takes an expectation over random transitions.
Q: Describe value iteration. => Repeat J ← min_u [g + E J(next)] until convergence.
Q: Describe policy iteration. => Alternate policy evaluation (solve for J^π) and policy improvement (greedy w.r.t. J^π).
Q: What is a Markov decision process? => States, actions, transition probabilities, rewards/costs, discount.
X: Run value iteration on a 4x4 gridworld with a goal and a pit.
R: With randomness, DP uses expectations; value and policy iteration are the foundation for RL algorithms.
## 10
topics: Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman, Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs and reachability analysis
read: PoRA Chapter 17; AA203 slides Lecture 10 and code
Q: What is the HJB equation? => The continuous-time Bellman equation: -∂V/∂t = min_u [L + ∇V^T f].
Q: What does reachability analysis compute? => The set of states from which a system can/must reach (or avoid) a target set under dynamics and disturbances.
Q: What does HJI add? => A second, adversarial player (disturbance) for worst-case guarantees.
Q: Why do safety teams care? => Reachability can prove a robot will never enter a dangerous state.
X: For a 1D system x_dot = u, |u| ≤ 1, describe the set of states that can reach x=0 within 2 s.
R: HJB/HJI give optimality and safety guarantees via reachability.
## 11
topics: Introduction to model predictive control
read: PoRA Chapter 3 Closed-Loop Control; AA203 slides Lecture 11
Q: Describe the MPC loop. => At each step solve a finite-horizon optimal control problem from the current state, apply the first input, then repeat.
Q: Why does MPC re-plan at every step? => Feedback from new measurements corrects model errors and disturbances.
Q: What are MPC's advantages over LQR? => It handles constraints (input limits, safety) explicitly.
Q: What is the receding horizon? => The prediction window moves forward with time.
X: Write the MPC optimization for a cart-pole with a force limit |u| ≤ 10.
R: MPC = repeated finite-horizon optimization with feedback; the method behind most real autonomous systems.
## 12
topics: Persistent feasibility of MPC, stability, explicit MPC
read: PoRA Chapter 3; AA203 slides Lecture 12
Q: What is persistent (recursive) feasibility? => If the MPC problem is feasible now, it stays feasible at all future steps.
Q: How do terminal constraints/costs help? => A terminal invariant set and terminal cost can guarantee feasibility and stability.
Q: What is explicit MPC? => Precomputing the piecewise-affine control law offline so online evaluation is a lookup.
Q: Why is recursive feasibility essential in practice? => An infeasible optimization during operation leaves the robot with no command.
X: List two ways you could make an MPC controller fall back safely if the solver fails.
R: Stability and feasibility of MPC need terminal ingredients; explicit MPC trades memory for speed. This closes Month 4.

Q: Define value V^π(s) and Q^π(s,a). => Expected return from s following π; expected return after taking a in s then following π.

## 17
topics: Model-free RL - value-based methods
read: PoRA Chapter 18; AA203 slides Lecture 17
Q: What is Q-learning? => Off-policy TD learning of Q* with target r + γ max_a' Q(s',a').
Q: Why can Q-learning learn from old data? => It's off-policy: updates don't require data from the current policy.
Q: What stabilizes deep Q-networks? => Replay buffers and target networks.
Q: What is overestimation bias? => Max over noisy estimates is biased high; Double Q-learning mitigates it.
X: Run tabular Q-learning on Gymnasium's FrozenLake.
R: Value-based methods learn Q and act greedily; off-policy and sample-efficient but can be unstable with function approximation.
## 18
topics: RL policy optimization
read: PoRA Chapter 18; AA203 slides Lecture 18
Q: State the policy gradient theorem. => ∇J = E[∇ log π(a|s) · Q^π(s,a)].
Q: Why use a baseline? => It reduces gradient variance without bias.
Q: What problem does PPO address? => Large policy updates that destroy performance; PPO clips the probability ratio.
Q: Why does the article note PPO is so popular? => Simple, stable and works across tasks (Gammelli calls it probably the most popular RL algorithm).
X: Write the PPO clipped objective and explain the role of epsilon.
R: Policy optimization directly adjusts the policy with gradient estimates; PPO is the standard.
