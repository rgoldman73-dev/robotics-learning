Q: What is behavior cloning's loss for continuous actions? => Typically mean squared error (or negative log-likelihood) between policy and expert actions.
Q: Why can a deterministic MSE policy fail with multimodal demonstrations? => It averages modes (e.g. left and right around an obstacle) into an invalid action.
Q: How do diffusion policies or action chunking help? => They model multimodal action distributions / predict sequences of actions for smoother, consistent behavior.

R: Imitation learning scales with demonstrations; multimodality and distribution shift are the main challenges.
## 3
read: Sutton & Barto policy-gradient chapter; Williams (1992) REINFORCE (listed on the course page)
Q: What is REINFORCE? => Monte-Carlo policy gradient: ∇J ≈ Σ ∇log π(a|s) · return.
Q: Why is it high variance? => Returns are noisy sums over entire trajectories.
Q: How to reduce variance? => Baselines, reward-to-go, and critics (next lecture).
X: Implement REINFORCE on CartPole-v1 with Gymnasium (or reproduce a simple sketch).
R: Policy gradients directly optimize expected return using sampled trajectories.
## 4
read: Schulman et al. PPO (2017) listed on the course page
Q: What does an actor-critic do? => The actor (policy) is updated using advantages estimated by a learned critic (value function).
Q: What is the advantage function? => A(s,a) = Q(s,a) − V(s): how much better the action is than average.
Q: Why does the critic reduce variance? => Bootstrapped value estimates replace noisy Monte-Carlo returns, at the cost of some bias.
X: Write the TD error δ = r + γV(s') − V(s) and explain how it estimates the advantage.
R: Actor-critic combines policy gradient with learned value baselines; basis for PPO.

## 7
read: Kostrikov et al. IQL (2021) listed on the course page
Q: What is offline RL? => Learning a policy from a fixed dataset without further environment interaction.
Q: Why is it hard? => Distribution shift: the policy may choose actions absent from data, where Q-values are overestimated.
Q: How do conservative methods help? => They constrain the policy to the data (or penalize unseen actions).
Q: Why does the article highlight offline RL for robots? => Robot time is expensive and data is collected once and reused.
X: Explain why vanilla Q-learning with max over actions fails with a small fixed dataset.
R: Offline RL learns from logged data; the key problem is out-of-distribution actions.
## 11
read: Janner et al. MBPO (2019) listed on the course page
Q: What does model-based RL learn? => A dynamics model s' = f(s,a) used for planning or synthetic rollouts.
Q: Why use short model rollouts (MBPO)? => Model errors compound over long horizons.
Q: What is model exploitation? => The policy/planner finds actions that exploit model errors, performing badly in reality.
X: Compare in two sentences model-free vs model-based RL for a real robot arm.
R: Model-based RL is more sample efficient but needs care about model bias.
