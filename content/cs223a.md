## 2
topics: Spatial descriptions, generalized coordinates, operational coordinates, rotation matrix, translations, homogeneous transform, operators
read: SEE "Kinematics-1" handout (Lectures 1-3); PoRA Chapter 1 Modeling Robot Dynamics (background)
Q: What does a rotation matrix describe? => The orientation of one coordinate frame relative to another; its columns are the axes of the rotated frame expressed in the reference frame.
Q: Name the key properties of a rotation matrix. => Orthonormal columns (R^T R = I), determinant +1, inverse equals transpose.
Q: What is a homogeneous transformation matrix? => A 4x4 matrix [R p; 0 1] combining rotation R and translation p so that transforms compose by matrix multiplication.
Q: What is the difference between generalized (joint) and operational (task) coordinates? => Joint coordinates are the robot's joint variables; operational coordinates describe the end-effector's position/orientation in task space.
X: Write the 4x4 transform for a frame rotated 90 degrees about z and translated by (1,2,0); apply it to the point (1,0,0).
R: Frames are described by rotation + position, packaged as a homogeneous transform. Everything in kinematics is composing such transforms.
## 3
topics: Homogeneous transform interpretations, compound transformations, rotation representations, Euler angles, fixed angles, example singularities, Euler parameters
read: SEE "Kinematics-1" handout (Lectures 1-3); Assignment 1
Q: In what three ways can a homogeneous transform be interpreted? => As a description of a frame, as a mapping of points between frames, and as an operator that moves points within one frame.
Q: What does composing T_AB and T_BC give? => T_AC = T_AB T_BC.
Q: Why does order of rotations matter? => Rotations about different axes do not commute.
Q: What is a singularity of Euler-angle representations (gimbal lock)? => A configuration where two rotation axes align and one degree of freedom is lost, so angles become non-unique.
Q: Why are Euler parameters (unit quaternions) used? => Four numbers represent any rotation without singularities.
X: In NumPy compose rotations Rz(30°)Rx(45°) and Rx(45°)Rz(30°); show they differ.
R: Rotations can be represented by matrices, Euler/fixed angles or quaternions; angle-triple representations have singularities.
## 4
topics: Manipulator kinematics, link description, link connections, Denavit-Hartenberg parameters, DH table, forward kinematics
read: SEE "Kinematics-2" handout (Lectures 4-5)
Q: What is forward kinematics? => Computing the end-effector pose from the joint variables.
Q: What are the four DH parameters for a link? => Link length a, link twist alpha, link offset d, joint angle theta.
Q: Which DH parameter is the variable for a revolute joint? => theta; for a prismatic joint it is d.
Q: How do you get the full arm transform from DH? => Multiply the per-link transforms: T_0N = T_01 T_12 ... T_(N-1)N.
X: Write the DH table for a planar 2-link arm with link lengths L1, L2.
R: DH parameters describe each link with four numbers; chaining link transforms yields forward kinematics.

## 6
topics: Instantaneous kinematics, Jacobian, direct differentiation, basic Jacobian, position representations, cross product operator, velocity propagation
read: SEE "Jacobian" handout (Lectures 6-8)
Q: What does the Jacobian relate? => Joint velocities to end-effector linear and angular velocities: v = J(q) q_dot.
Q: Name two ways to compute the Jacobian from the lecture. => Direct differentiation of position kinematics, and velocity propagation (basic Jacobian) using cross products.
Q: What is the cross-product (skew-symmetric) operator for? => It writes w × r as a matrix multiplication, handy in velocity propagation.
Q: What is the shape of the Jacobian for a 6-DOF arm in 3D? => 6 x 6 (rows: 3 linear + 3 angular velocity; columns: joints).
X: Derive the 2x2 Jacobian of the planar 2-link arm by differentiating (x,y) with respect to q1,q2.
R: The Jacobian maps joint rates to hand rates; it can be derived by differentiation or by propagating velocities link to link.
## 7
topics: Jacobian explicit form, Jv/Jw, Jacobian in a frame, Scheinman arm Jacobian, kinematic singularity
read: SEE "Jacobian" handout (Lectures 6-8); Assignment 3
Q: What is a kinematic singularity? => A configuration where the Jacobian loses rank, so the hand cannot move in some direction.
Q: How can you detect a singularity numerically? => det(J)=0 (square J) or a very small smallest singular value.
Q: Why does the frame in which the Jacobian is expressed matter? => J^0 and J^i differ by rotations; pick the frame appropriate for your task.
X: Find the singular configurations of the planar 2-link arm (q2=0 or π) and explain why.
R: The Jacobian can be expressed in different frames; where it loses rank are singularities.
## 8
topics: Scheinman arm demo, kinematic singularity example, PUMA simulation, resolved rate motion control, velocity/force duality, virtual work
read: SEE "Jacobian" handout (Lectures 6-8); Assignments 3-4
Q: What is resolved-rate motion control? => Commanding joint velocities q_dot = J^{-1} x_dot to produce a desired hand velocity.
Q: What happens near a singularity? => J^{-1} blows up and joint velocities become huge.
Q: State the velocity/force duality. => Forces map from hand to joints through J^T: tau = J^T F.
Q: What principle gives tau = J^T F? => Virtual work: power in joint space equals power at the hand.
X: Compute the joint torques needed for a 2-link arm to push with 5 N along x at q=(45°,45°).
R: J maps velocities forward; J^T maps forces backward (statics). Resolved-rate control inverts J and fails at singularities.
## 9

read: SEE "Robots and Vision" handout (Lecture 9); PoRA Chapters 6-7 (camera models, stereo)
Q: How does stereo vision recover depth? => By matching points in two images and triangulating from their disparity.
Q: What is a disparity map? => An image of per-pixel horizontal shifts between left and right views; larger disparity means closer.
Q: What is SIFT used for? => Detecting and describing local image features that are robust to scale and rotation, for matching.
Q: What is a "tracking cycle" in visual servoing? => Repeating: acquire image, extract features, update estimate, command the robot.
X: Sketch the geometry of two pinhole cameras viewing one point and mark the baseline and disparity.
R: Vision gives robots 3D information through stereo and feature tracking; it links to Month 3.
## 10

read: SEE "Trajectory Planning" handout (Lecture 10); Assignment 4
Q: Why plan trajectories rather than jump to a goal? => To get smooth motion with bounded velocity/acceleration that the actuators can follow.
Q: What boundary conditions determine a cubic joint trajectory? => Start/end position and velocity (4 conditions for 4 coefficients).
Q: Why use higher-order polynomials or via points? => To also match accelerations or pass through intermediate configurations.
Q: Joint space vs Cartesian planning? => Joint space is simple and avoids IK per step; Cartesian gives straight-line hand paths but may hit singularities.
X: Compute the cubic from q(0)=0 to q(1)=1 with zero start/end velocity.
R: Smooth polynomial trajectories connect via points; planning in joint or Cartesian space has trade-offs.
## 11
topics: Joint-space dynamics, Newton-Euler algorithm, inertia tensor, Newton-Euler equations, Lagrange equations, equations of motion
read: SEE "Dynamics" handout (Lectures 11-12); PoRA Chapter 1 Modeling Robot Dynamics
Q: What do the dynamics of a manipulator describe? => The relationship between joint torques and joint accelerations/velocities.
Q: What is the Newton-Euler approach? => Write force/moment balance for each link and propagate forward (velocities) then backward (forces).
Q: What is the inertia tensor? => A 3x3 matrix capturing how mass is distributed, relating angular velocity to angular momentum.
Q: Lagrange vs Newton-Euler? => Lagrange uses energy (kinetic minus potential) and yields closed-form equations; Newton-Euler is recursive and efficient to compute.
X: Derive the equation of motion of a single pendulum with Lagrange: m L² θ¨ + m g L sinθ = τ.
R: Dynamics can be derived via Newton-Euler or Lagrange; both give the same equations of motion.
## 12
topics: Lagrange equations, equations of motion, kinetic energy, explicit form, centrifugal and Coriolis forces, Christoffel symbols, mass matrix, V matrix, final equation of motion
read: SEE "Dynamics" handout (Lectures 11-12); Assignment 5
Q: Write the standard manipulator equation of motion. => A(q) q¨ + b(q,q˙) + g(q) = τ (mass matrix, Coriolis/centrifugal, gravity).
Q: Why does the mass matrix depend on configuration? => Effective inertia at each joint changes with the arm's pose.
Q: Where do Coriolis/centrifugal terms come from? => Velocity-dependent terms from differentiating the configuration-dependent kinetic energy (Christoffel symbols).
Q: Why do motors need different torques at different poses? => Gravity torque and effective inertia depend on q.
X: For the 2-link planar arm, write A(q) and note which entries depend on q2.
R: Dynamics: A(q)q¨ + b + g = τ with configuration-dependent inertia; the foundation of model-based control.
## 13
topics: Control overview, joint space control, resolved motion rate control, natural systems, dissipative systems, passive system stability
read: SEE "Control" handout (Lectures 13-15); Assignment 5
Q: What is joint-space control? => Controlling each joint to track a desired joint trajectory.
Q: Why do dissipative (damped) systems settle? => Energy decreases through damping, so the system moves toward its minimum-energy equilibrium.
Q: What does passivity/dissipation buy for stability? => Adding damping to a potential-shaping controller guarantees stability by Lyapunov-style energy arguments.
X: Simulate a damped mass-spring and show energy decreasing.
R: Control can be viewed as shaping energy: a spring toward the goal plus damping gives stability.
## 14
topics: PD control, control partitioning, motion control, disturbance rejection, steady-state error, PID control, effective inertia
read: SEE "Control" handout (Lectures 13-15); Assignment 6
Q: What does a PD controller compute? => τ = Kp (q_d - q) - Kd q_dot.
Q: What does the integral term in PID fix? => Steady-state error from constant disturbances such as gravity.
Q: What is control partitioning (computed torque)? => Split into a model-based part that cancels dynamics and a servo part that controls a unit-mass system.
Q: Why does effective inertia matter in gain choice? => Natural frequency depends on stiffness/inertia, and inertia varies with pose.
X: Tune Kp, Kd for a unit-mass system to get critical damping (Kd = 2 sqrt(Kp)).
R: PD/PID plus model-based decoupling gives accurate joint-space motion control.
## 15
topics: Manipulator control, PD control stability, task-oriented control, task-oriented equations of motion, operational space dynamics, nonlinear dynamic decoupling, trajectory tracking
read: SEE "Control" handout (Lectures 13-15); Assignment 6
Q: What is operational-space control? => Control formulated directly in task coordinates (e.g. hand position) using task-space dynamics.
Q: What is the operational-space mass matrix Λ(x)? => The effective inertia felt at the hand, Λ = (J A^{-1} J^T)^{-1}.
Q: What is nonlinear dynamic decoupling? => Using the model to cancel coupling/nonlinearities so each task coordinate behaves like an independent unit mass.
Q: What is the advantage of operational-space control for tasks? => Direct control of what matters (hand motion/force) without solving IK each step.
X: List which terms of the operational-space control law F = Λ F* + μ + p are model-based and which are the servo.
R: Operational-space control = decoupled task-space dynamics + a simple servo, central to Khatib's framework.
