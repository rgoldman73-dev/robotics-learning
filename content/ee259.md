Q: What does an accelerometer measure? => Specific force (acceleration minus gravity) along its sensing axis.
Q: Describe the basic proof-mass principle. => A mass on a spring deflects under acceleration; the deflection is sensed (e.g. capacitively) and scaled to acceleration.
Q: Why does an accelerometer at rest read about 9.8 m/s^2? => It senses the reaction to gravity (specific force).
X: Read the 3 axes of your phone's accelerometer lying flat and tilted (any sensor-logging app) and note the numbers.
R: Accelerometers measure specific force via a proof mass; gravity is part of the reading.

Q: What does a gyroscope measure? => Angular rate about its axis.
Q: How do MEMS gyros work (concept)? => A vibrating structure experiences Coriolis force when rotated; this is sensed to infer angular rate.
Q: What is gyro bias and why does it matter? => A constant offset in output that, when integrated, causes orientation error growing with time.
X: Integrate a constant 0.01 rad/s bias for 60 s in Python and see the resulting angle error.
R: Gyros give angular rate; integrating rate gives orientation but also integrates bias and noise.
## 7
read: PoRA Chapters 5 and 11 (filtering intro)
Q: What is dead reckoning? => Estimating position by integrating motion measurements from a known start.
Q: Why does inertial navigation drift? => Noise and bias get integrated, once for velocity and twice for position, so errors grow quickly.
Q: How can drift be controlled? => Fuse with absolute references (GPS, vision, lidar) in a filter.
X: Simulate double integration of noisy accelerometer data for a stationary sensor and plot the position error.
R: IMU-only navigation drifts; sensor fusion is needed for long-term accuracy.

Q: What does lidar measure? => Range to surfaces, by emitting laser pulses (or modulated light) and timing/phase of the reflection.
Q: Time-of-flight formula? => Range = c * Δt / 2.
Q: Why do lidar and cameras fail differently? => Lidar gives direct range but struggles with glass/fog and sparse data; cameras give dense texture but no direct depth and suffer from lighting.
X: Compute the round-trip time for a target at 30 m.
R: Lidar measures geometry via light time-of-flight; learn its physics and failure modes.

Q: What is the pinhole camera model? => Maps 3D points to 2D pixels by perspective projection x = f X/Z, y = f Y/Z.
Q: What are camera intrinsics vs extrinsics? => Intrinsics: focal length, principal point, distortion. Extrinsics: camera pose in the world.
Q: How does a lens change the pinhole model? => It gathers more light and focuses it, introducing distortion and depth of field.
